### Executive Summary

This report draws on operational data from over 380 distributed energy sites across sub-Saharan Africa, alongside survey responses from 94 energy operators, O&M contractors, and utility grid managers. Our goal: to establish a baseline for how the industry currently manages distributed renewable assets — and where the gaps are widest.

The headline finding is stark: the median energy operator has visibility into less than 60% of their installed asset fleet in real time. The remaining 40% is managed reactively — faults discovered after the fact, maintenance triggered by customer complaints, and performance data assembled from monthly vendor exports.

### Key findings

- 1. Tooling fragmentation is the primary operational challenge78% of respondents use more than three separate tools to manage their asset portfolio. 34% use five or more. The most commonly cited pain point is the absence of a single performance comparison view across sites — operators cannot easily answer the question "which of my sites is underperforming?" without significant manual effort.
- 2. Field operations are the biggest efficiency gapMean time to repair (MTTR) across surveyed O&M contractors averages 4.2 days for inverter faults and 6.8 days for battery system issues. First-time fix rates average 61% — meaning nearly 4 in 10 field dispatches require a second visit. The primary causes cited: inadequate pre-visit diagnostics and incomplete asset documentation.
- 3. Investor reporting remains manual at most operatorsOnly 23% of respondents have automated investor reporting workflows. The majority still compile monthly performance reports manually, with an average of 2.4 full working days per report per portfolio. As portfolios scale, this cost scales linearly — and introduces significant risk of error.

### Implications and recommendations

The data points to a clear operational maturity gap in the African distributed energy sector — one that is becoming more costly as portfolio sizes grow. Our recommendations:

- Prioritize unified telemetry infrastructure before expanding portfolio size. Operators who attempt to scale past 8 sites without a consolidated monitoring layer report significantly higher operational costs per site.
- Invest in mobile-first field tools that work offline. Remote site connectivity remains a persistent challenge — tools that require constant internet connectivity fail regularly in the field.
- Automate investor reporting from live data. The manual reporting burden is not a scaling problem; it is a tooling problem with a direct solution.

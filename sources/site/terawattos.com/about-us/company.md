# We Exist To Help Manage Your Renewable Energy Assets

Terawatt was founded by energy operators who experienced the operational pain of fragmented tools, siloed SCADA systems, and manual processes. We built the solution we could not find anywhere else.

#### 

our story

“We spent years watching the renewable energy industry manage billion-dollar infrastructure with tools built for something else entirely.“

Terawatt was founded in 2025 by a team of energy operators, embedded systems engineers, and enterprise software builders who had worked across solar, wind, and battery storage deployments in Nigeria. We had seen the same dysfunction repeat itself across every organization we worked with.

Excellent renewable energy assets, well-designed, well-financed, well-intentioned being let down at the operational layer. Faults going undetected for weeks. Field technicians arriving on-site without the right information. Investors receiving performance reports assembled from manual exports and gut-checks. The infrastructure was getting smarter. The operations layer was not keeping pace.

We didn't build Terawatt because we saw a market opportunity. We built it because we were the people who needed it and it didn't exist. That's still the clearest way we can explain why we are here.

WHAT WE STAND FOR

## Four principles that shape every decision we make.

#### Operators first, always.

Every decision starts by asking: does this make an energy operator's life meaningfully better? We don't build for features we build for workflows.

#### Lean teams, big portfolios

We exist to give small O&M teams the leverage to manage large, complex portfolios. Efficiency isn't a feature, it's our purpose.

#### Vendor-agnostic by principle.

We will never favor one manufacturer equipment over another. Our platform work equally well regardless of who made your assets.

#### The transition needs speed.

Downtime is expensive. Every minute a fault goes undetected costs real money. We build for speed in detection, in alerting, in resolution.
